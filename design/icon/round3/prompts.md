# yebeyt icon — round 3: Apple-quality, unmistakably Persian literature

History: round 1 (neon outlines) was rejected as cheap. Round 2 (Apple iOS 26 glass style) was liked for its quality, but the user says it does not show Persian literature: a crescent, a book or a chat bubble could be any app. This round must keep round 2's quality AND make Persian poetry obvious at first glance.

Shared style (apply to every image):
- Apple first-party iOS 26 icon quality, Liquid Glass / layered: one bold central composition, centred with optical balance, about 60% of the canvas, generous even margins.
- 2–3 stacked layers of real material: frosted translucent turquoise glass with a crisp specular rim light on top edges, subtle refraction where layers overlap, soft realistic contact shadow. Light from the top. Perfectly smooth studio render, no grain.
- Smooth background gradient, no vignette, no noise, no scattered stars: deep ink-navy #0B1426 at the bottom to deep teal #0E3A44 at the top.
- Colours: Persian turquoise #2EC4D6 (highlights #7FE3EC–#C9F6FA, shadows #128A9A), warm white, and a SMALL accent of soft burnished gold like Persian manuscript illumination (tazhib). Gold is an accent only.
- Persian elements must be authentic: real Persian nastaliq calligraphy shapes and real Persian manuscript/tile ornament, not fake squiggles or Arabic-looking generic script.
- Square 1024×1024, full-bleed background, do NOT draw rounded corners or a frame around the whole image, no Latin letters, no device or mockup.

## A — glass card with nastaliq ب
Two rounded-rectangle glass cards, portrait, stacked: a back card in deeper translucent teal tilted about 8° left, a front card in frosted pale turquoise glass tilted about 4° right. On the front card, a single large Persian letter "ب" in elegant nastaliq calligraphy (long sweeping horizontal bowl, thin to thick stroke, one diamond-shaped dot below), raised in warm white glass. A thin delicate gold illuminated border (tazhib-style vine line) runs just inside the front card's edge.

## B — Divan page
An open Persian manuscript (divan of poetry) seen slightly from above, made of frosted glass pages. Each page shows the classic Persian poetry layout: two columns of short horizontal lines (the two hemistichs of each couplet), rendered as abstract soft white strokes, not readable text, with a narrow gutter between the columns. At the top of the pages, a small turquoise-and-gold illuminated headpiece (sarlowh) with a tiny shamseh medallion. Bold, simple silhouette that still reads at small size.

## C — the word «بیت» in nastaliq glass
The Persian word «بیت» (beyt, meaning "couplet") written once in beautiful, correct Persian nastaliq calligraphy, rendered as a thick, solid piece of frosted turquoise glass with a warm white specular top rim, floating with a soft shadow. Correct letters right-to-left: ب joined to ی joined to ت, with one dot under ب, two dots under ی, two dots above ت. Behind it, very subtly, a faint large shamseh (Persian sun medallion) in deeper teal glass. The word is the hero; nothing else.

## D — reed pen writing ب
A Persian calligraphy reed pen (qalam) made of polished turquoise glass with a small gold band, angled diagonally from top-right, its nib finishing a single bold nastaliq "ب" stroke in luminous warm-white glass; the ب's dot below is a small round gold ink drop. Clean, iconic, three elements only.
