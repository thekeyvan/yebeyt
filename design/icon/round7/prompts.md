# yebeyt icon — round 7: Simurgh that says "poetry", matte

Feedback on round 6 (attached reference = the Simurgh the user liked): the user likes the Simurgh and prefers the MATTE finish, but says none of the icons say anything about poetry. This round: every icon must signal **poetry / verse / writing** at first glance, without using letters, and most are built around the Simurgh.

ABSOLUTE RULE — NO RELIGION, NO POLITICS. None of: crescent moons, moon+star, Faravahar or winged discs, mosque domes/minarets/arches, crosses, Star of David, prayer hands, religious calligraphy, Lion and Sun, flags, crowns, eagles as national emblems. No letters, words or text of any script.

The Simurgh (when used): Persian-miniature-inspired mythic bird simplified into a modern mark: slim elegant body, small head with a long curling crest, and long flowing ribbon-like tail feathers with softly curled tips. NOT a phoenix in flames, NOT an eagle, NOT a peacock (no eye-spots).

Finish "MATTE" (every image): soft-touch matte 3D like ceramic or anodised aluminium, gentle diffuse studio light from the top, subtle gradients across surfaces, a thin crisp rim highlight on top edges only, soft contact shadow. Crisp, intentional vector-like curves. NOT glossy, NOT jelly, NOT candy, no big white specular blobs.

Shared rules:
- Background: deep ink navy #070F22 to #0B1426, smooth, no vignette, no scattered stars, no noise.
- Palette: turquoise #2EC4D6 hero (tints #5FF2FF, #C9F6FA, shade #128A9A), soft lilac #B79CFF and warm peach #FFB38A accents, tiny soft gold allowed.
- COMPACT composition: the mark fits a square, centred with optical balance, 60–70% of the canvas, never a wide horizontal strip. Reads at 40px.
- Square 1024×1024, full-bleed, do NOT draw rounded corners or a frame, no device or mockup.

## A — Simurgh feather quill
A single long Simurgh tail feather standing diagonally, its vane in turquoise shading into lilac and a peach curled tip, its lower end cut into a fine writing nib in soft gold, with one small round ink drop just below the nib. The feather is unmistakably a quill pen for writing verse, and unmistakably the Simurgh's feather (graceful curl, ribbon-like). One object.

## B — wings made of pages
A Simurgh rising upward, seen from the front, its two wings formed by the fanned-out pages of an open book (soft white-turquoise pages layered like feathers), its body and crest turquoise, a short lilac-and-peach ribbon tail below. Poetry taking flight. Compact, symmetrical, iconic.

## C — two Simurghs, one couplet
Two small Simurghs facing each other in mirror symmetry, their curling crests almost touching at the top and their long ribbon tails curving down and around to meet at the bottom, together forming a heart-like closed shape. One turquoise, one lilac. Two halves = the two lines of a couplet = two friends.

## D — tail of verses
A Simurgh perched and seen in profile, compact, its long tail made of exactly two parallel flowing ribbons that sweep forward and around under the bird like two lines of verse written in the air, one turquoise, one lilac, ending in small curls. The two-line tail is the hero.

## E — the poem card
A single upright matte card (rounded rectangle, portrait, like a poetry postcard from the app) in deep turquoise, slightly tilted, with a small embossed Simurgh in soft lilac and gold at its centre, and two short horizontal engraved lines beneath the bird like a couplet. A second card in lilac peeks out behind it.

## F — feather quotation marks
Two Simurgh tail feathers curled into the shape of the Persian quotation marks « » side by side, one turquoise, one lilac, with a tiny peach glint between them. Reads as "a quote", i.e. a verse, while being made entirely of the Simurgh's feathers.
