# yebeyt icon — round 9: say POETRY (beyt, mesra, ghazal), not "writing"

Feedback: the quill reads as a writing/notes app. The user wants the icon to be about Persian poetry itself: the couplet (beyt), its two half-lines (mesra), the ghazal, and the classic images of Persian poetry. The Simurgh with page-wings (attached reference) is liked. Same matte finish and palette (shared.md). No letters: show poetry through its SHAPE and its classic imagery.

Key visual idea used below — the shape of a Persian couplet: a Persian beyt is written as TWO equal half-lines SIDE BY SIDE on one row, with a clear gap in the middle (unlike Western poems, which stack lines). A ghazal is a column of such rows. Draw half-lines as smooth rounded matte bars of equal length, never as letters or scribbles.

## A — Simurgh on a beyt
The gentle-but-proud Simurgh from the reference (crested, ribbon tail), small and elegant, perched on the right one of two equal horizontal matte bars that sit side by side on one row with a gap between them, like a bird on a line of verse. The two bars are the two mesras of one beyt: one turquoise, one lilac. The bird's lilac-and-peach ribbon tail hangs down gracefully. Bold and simple.

## B — candle and moth (sham-o-parvaneh)
The classic love image of Persian ghazals: a short, chunky matte candle in pale turquoise-white with a soft rounded turquoise flame, and a lilac moth with rounded wings circling close to the flame, a faint dotted orbit path curving around it. Warm, romantic, simple.

## C — the beyt card
A single upright matte card in deep turquoise (rounded rectangle, portrait, slightly tilted), with a lilac card peeking behind it. On the front card, two rows of verse in the Persian couplet layout: each row is two equal rounded bars side by side with a gap in the middle (four bars total, in soft white), and above them a small embossed Simurgh in lilac and gold. Reads instantly as "a Persian poem on a card".

## D — Simurgh with couplet wings
The page-wing Simurgh of the reference, front view, but each wing is now made of three broad horizontal feather-bars stacked like rows of verse, and the left wing and right wing together form the two half-lines of each row (mirror images with the bird's body as the gap in the middle). Turquoise-white feathers, lilac and peach ribbon tail. Symmetrical and bold.

## E — ghazal with a rhyme
A column of three rows in the Persian couplet layout (each row: two equal rounded matte bars side by side with a gap), in soft white-turquoise, and at the left end of every row a matching small round gem in peach (the repeating rhyme, radif, of a ghazal). A tiny Simurgh feather in lilac curls over the top row. Abstract but unmistakably a Persian ghazal page.

## F — the two-mesra feathers
Two long Simurgh tail feathers lying horizontally side by side on one row with a small gap between their tips, like the two mesras of a beyt: the right one turquoise, the left one lilac, both curling up softly at their outer ends with peach tips. Above them, a single small peach spark. Elegant, logo-like.
