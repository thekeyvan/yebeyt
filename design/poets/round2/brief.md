# yebeyt · poet portraits, round 2 (simple + bold)

Round 1 (../poet-*.png) had the right STYLE but the faces were not like the poets: the classical poets all came out as the same generic bearded man. This round is about LIKENESS.

## Likeness (most important)
- Each portrait must look like the image people already know for that poet:
  - Classical poets: follow their famous, widely reproduced traditional portraits (the ones on book covers, tomb murals, banknotes and stamps in Iran). Keep each poet's own face shape, age, beard shape and clothing from that familiar image.
  - Modern poets: follow their best-known photographs closely (face shape, hairline, glasses, moustache).
- Every poet must be clearly different from the others at a glance (age, face shape, beard length and shape, headwear, pose).

## Headwear
- Draw the headwear the poet wears in their familiar portrait (for classical poets this is usually a simple wrapped cloth headwrap or a cap). Keep it plain: no emblems, no badges, no religious symbols, no tall dervish hat, no halo, no calligraphy, no crescent or star.

## Style (identical for every image) — SIMPLE and BOLD
- Very simple, bold line art, like an app avatar icon: the FEWEST lines that still make the poet recognisable.
- One thick, even stroke weight: about 22 px at 1024 px. Rounded caps and joins. Black #000000 lines only.
- Minimal detail: no individual hair strands, no beard texture lines, no wrinkles, no fabric folds, no eyelashes. Hair, beard and headwrap are simple closed outlines with at most 1–2 inner lines. Eyes are simple dots or short curves.
- Pure white #FFFFFF background, no fill, no shading, no grey, no colour.
- Head and shoulders, centred, small in the frame: the whole figure fits inside a centred circle of about 70% of the canvas width, with lots of empty margin all around (it will be shown inside a small circle, so nothing may come near the edges).
- 1024x1024 square PNG. No text, letters, names, signature, watermark, frame or circle drawn.
